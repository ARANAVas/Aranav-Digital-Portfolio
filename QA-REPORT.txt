ARANAV PORTFOLIO — V9 RECRUITER-READY QA REPORT
Date: 04 November 2026
Package: White + Crimson Red default theme; cinematic dark alternate

SUMMARY
V9 refines the portfolio's photo storytelling, skill-door interaction, typography and recruiter access. The white + crimson-red look shown in the supplied reference screenshot is now the first-visit default; the dark theme remains available through the theme toggle. The awkward mini portrait stickers have been removed from the main image and replaced with a balanced two-card gallery beneath it. The resume is a one-column ATS-friendly PDF with an editable DOCX counterpart.

VISUAL & UX CHANGES
1. PHOTO PRESENTATION
   - The main story/workspace portrait is shown without floating profile stickers.
   - The two formal portraits are placed in equal, aligned editorial cards below the main image.
   - The fourth photo remains in the hero, giving all four photos distinct roles across the site.
   - Desktop story composition was visually inspected; portrait cards sit below the main photo with a measured 13px gap in the tested layout.
2. SKILL-DOOR ANIMATION
   - Four doors reveal a recessed portal with a visible inner frame and plain reverse panel.
   - Door panels use a 3D hinge swing with weighted easing; front-facing labels fade during rotation to avoid mirrored text.
   - The detail-panel reveal is synchronised with the 1.18-second door transition.
   - Mid-opening desktop capture was visually inspected.
3. TYPE & RECRUITER ACCESS
   - Manrope is used for UI/body copy, Cormorant Garamond for editorial emphasis, and DM Mono for compact metadata.
   - HTML font imports and CSS font variables now match; system fallbacks remain available.
   - A direct View resume action is available in the hero.
4. ATS RESUME
   - Rebuilt as a clean one-column, text-first, one-page A4 PDF and editable one-page DOCX.
   - No photograph, sidebar, complex tables or text boxes; contact links remain clickable.
   - Experience and skills are described without invented metrics, awards or certifications. Internship date ranges were not supplied, so no dates were invented.
